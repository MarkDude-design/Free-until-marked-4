**Title:** Who Is Driving? Authority Needs a Return Path

**Short description:** A spectacularly unsuitable bus driver makes a practical point: raw capability is not role-fit, and delegated authority needs a visible way back to the human.

## Abstract

A machine volunteers to drive the bus. Its confidence is impressive; its suitability for the job is another question. Starting with that comic failure, this talk examines a familiar engineering mistake: assigning authority because a system appears capable, without making its role, limits and return path clear. The bus turns an abstract delegation problem into something concrete. Who granted the authority? What can the system actually do? What happens when it fails, and how does control return? Historical computer imagery and a small everyday-device comparison keep the discussion grounded. The aim is a practical habit for automation work: choose the right role, make consequential authority visible, and know how the human gets the wheel back.

## Attendees will learn to

- Distinguish raw capability from suitability for a particular role.
- Identify the authority, failure behavior and return path in an automation proposal.
- Ask for a concrete human-control mechanism rather than treating redundant tools as a complete answer.
