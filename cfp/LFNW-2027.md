## Proposal hook

A bus crashes. Who was driving, who gave permission—and how do we get home?

## Long abstract (ready to paste)

AI agents can increasingly take action on behalf of people. The interesting question is not just whether they can steer. It is whether the tool fits the role, whether its authority is legible, and whether anyone can take back the wheel.

This short, visual talk uses a bus and a crash to teach a practical model of delegation: explicitly name what was allowed; give the tool only the needed resources; preserve an audit trail; and identify a real return path when things go wrong. Human approval and a STOP signal are useful only if the action boundary actually honors them.

The goal is not to dramatize an imaginary rogue machine. It is to give open-source builders and curious beginners an understandable way to inspect an automation: Who is driving? Under whose authority? What happens when the driver fails? The same questions apply to scripts, bots, and AI assistants.

## Three takeaways

- Capability does not equal permission or role-fit.
- An observable, enforceable stop boundary beats a theatrical emergency cord.
- Audit logs and human revocation belong in the design.
